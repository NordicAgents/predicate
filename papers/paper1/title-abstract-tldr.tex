\title{Conflict-Complete Retrieval for Agent Memory: Minimal Witnesses under Bounded Context}

\begin{abstract}
An agent cannot reason about a contradiction that retrieval never surfaces. We treat silent conflict
selection in long-term agent memory as a retrieval-completeness problem and make it precise. We define
\emph{conflict completeness} for a retrieval policy under a bounded context budget: whenever a
query-relevant conflict exists in the store, retrieval must return a complete, source-grounded witness
that a sound detector can verify from the returned context alone. We prove that policies determined
only by IRI adjacency are conflict-incomplete for any finite hop bound when the conflicting records are
connected solely through a shared key \emph{literal}. This regime is not hypothetical: in a dated
export of a public vulnerability database, 99.5\% of co-referent record pairs are linked only by a
shared identifier string, with no graph path between them. We then give the incremental \emph{Conflict
Witness Index} (CWI), which maintains key-induced equivalence and returns minimal witnesses for a
bounded rule fragment with valid-time and scope side conditions, at a context cost equal to the witness
itself (2--12 triples) and independent of the graph distance between the records.

We are explicit about where this wins and where it does not. On five of seven benchmark families a
\emph{key-aware} neighborhood policy is already witness-complete at 4--12 triples, within roughly
$1.3\times$ of CWI; the witness index earns its cost only when records are co-referent through a
\emph{chain} of key hops, where a neighborhood policy either returns no witness at all below the chain
depth or must buy the whole radius-$m$ ball. In the vulnerability slice above such chains are only
0.5\% of co-referent pairs, so we do not claim the index is broadly necessary --- we claim the
\emph{property} is, and CWI is one implementation that attains its lower bound.

The stronger empirical result is that completeness alone is not sufficient. Feeding five hosted LLM
readers exactly what each policy returned, detection of a conflict whose full witness is present falls
from 0.94 when the returned context is under 10 triples to 0.59 at 250 triples or more, and the split
across models is stark: two readers are essentially unaffected ($1.00 \to 0.99$ and $0.99 \to 0.95$
from small to large contexts) while three degrade sharply (to 0.70, 0.54 and 0.22). Because a
deployment cannot know in advance which kind of reader it has, returning a minimal witness rather than
a merely sufficient neighborhood is a robustness property, not a byte-count optimization. When
retrieval returns only one side of a conflict, readers named a single value in 96.7\% of cases and
abstained once in 1{,}015 --- the silent-selection failure this paper is named for, measured rather than
assumed. Across stores up to 56{,}009 triples CWI sustains approximately 1.89 index writes per
assertion and 5--8\,\textmu s median query latency, and its complete witnesses cost at most
$3.08\times$ a flag-plus-pointer encoding while remaining independently checkable. Conflict
\emph{detection} in this fragment is a standard key join and we claim no advantage there. All
confirmatory results use synthetic fixtures under an oracle schema and establish the mechanism, not
external validity; the reader arm runs on hosted endpoints that are not version-pinned, and one model
was withdrawn by its vendor mid-experiment.
\end{abstract}

\paragraph{TL;DR.}
Retrieval, not reasoning, decides whether an agent can see a contradiction: IRI-adjacency retrieval
provably cannot reach records joined only by a shared key literal, and even when the full witness
\emph{is} returned, readers detect it 0.94 of the time in under 10 triples but only 0.59 at 250-plus ---
so agent memory should return minimal witnesses, which CWI does in 2--12 triples.
